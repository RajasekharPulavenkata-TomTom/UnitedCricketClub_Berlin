Rules of Cricket

The essential laws that govern the game of cricket.

Objective

Two teams of 11 players each compete over one or two innings. The batting team scores runs while the fielding team tries to dismiss all 10 batters (one must always be at the crease). The team with the most runs wins.

Ways to Get Out
  • Bowled — ball hits the stumps directly.
  • Caught — fielder catches the ball before it bounces.
  • LBW — ball hits the leg when it would have hit the stumps.
  • Run Out — batter is outside the crease when stumps are broken during a run.
  • Stumped — wicket-keeper breaks the stumps while batter is out of crease.
  • Hit Wicket — batter dislodges the stumps with bat or body.
  • Handled the Ball — batter deliberately handles the ball.
  • Obstructing the Field — batter deliberately hinders a fielder.
  • Timed Out — new batter takes more than 3 minutes to appear.
  • Hit the Ball Twice — batter strikes the ball twice intentionally.
Illegal Deliveries
  • Wide
    Ball passes too far from the batter to play. 1 run added to the batting team, delivery re-bowled.
  • No-Ball
    Bowler oversteps the crease, bowls above waist height (full toss), or throws instead of bowling. 1 run added, free hit follows in limited-overs cricket.
  • Free Hit
    After a no-ball in limited-overs cricket, the batter cannot be dismissed (except by run-out) on the next delivery.
  • Beamer
    Full-toss delivery above waist height. Automatically a no-ball; bowler receives a warning.
Scoring Runs
  • Running between wickets — both batters cross to opposite ends.
  • Boundary 4 — ball reaches the boundary after bouncing.
  • Six — ball clears the boundary without bouncing.
  • Extras — wides, no-balls, byes, and leg byes add runs without the bat.
DRS — Decision Review System

Each team gets 2 reviews per innings (3 in Tests). A review is lost if the on-field decision stands.

Technologies used: ball-tracking (Hawk-Eye), ultra-edge (Snickometer), hot spot, and slow-motion replay to challenge umpiring decisions.

Other Key Laws
  • Over — 6 legal deliveries bowled from one end. The same bowler cannot bowl consecutive overs.
  • Follow-on — in Tests, if a team's first innings deficit is ≥200 runs, the opposition can enforce the follow-on.
  • Declaration — the batting captain may declare the innings closed at any time in Tests and first-class cricket.
  • Bad Light / Rain — umpires may suspend play for poor light or rain. In limited-overs matches, D/L method recalculates targets.
  • Ball tampering — illegally altering the condition of the ball is an offence; 5 penalty runs awarded to the batting side.
